## Other notes

## Findings from web research
- Built by British Royal Engineers from 1930, completed about 1939, on roughly 8 ha (about 20 acres) (WARMAP, MSN).
- Garrison: British, Malay and Sikh soldiers; defenses included cannons, anti-aircraft guns and pillboxes (WARMAP).
- Japanese forces took Penang in December 1941 with little fighting; the site then served as a naval base and detention centre (WARMAP).
- Abandoned after the war, restored from the late 1990s by Johari Shafie, opened 2002 (WARMAP).
- Memorial sponsored by Penang War Veterans Association, completed 2003 (WARMAP).
- Gaps: no official site found; no dimensions; no site map; no photos of the command centre, observation tower or escape route; no licensed video.

## Web research round 2 (2026-11-24)
Source: https://thesmartlocal.my/penang-war-museum/ (travel site, medium reliability), plus search results.
- Practical info (check before use, may be outdated): address Jalan Batu Maung, 11960 Batu Maung, Pulau Pinang; hours 9am to 6pm daily; tickets listed as RM22 adult / RM12 child for Malaysians and RM38 / RM20 standard; phone 016-421 3606. The museum has a Facebook page and an Instagram account (the page links to them but I did not find the exact URLs; likely the official source for photos and permission requests).
- Observation post: described as having a 320 degree panoramic view over the Strait of Malacca (single source, unverified).
- Tunnels: described as leading to ammunition storage and the communication area; climbing down is advised with guides (single source).
- Guillotine: remains of a guillotine stand used by the Japanese are shown. Treat claims of casualty numbers on that page as unreliable (a figure of 12,000 POWs dying is probably confused with the Burma Railway).
- Penang fell to Japanese forces in December 1941 (WARMAP) or by January 1942 (this page); sources differ, so avoid giving an exact date in app content.
- Not found online: the location of the "To Our Glorious Dead" memorial on the site, and the "Escape route" sign. Search results for these were unrelated cenotaphs elsewhere. Need the user's screenshots, the museum's Facebook page or a site visit.

## Building research (2026-11-26)
Question: building dimensions and interiors. Finding: no measured drawings, plans or dimensions are published online. The model's sizes are inferred from the photos in `photos/` and from the standard layout of a 1930s British 6-inch BL coast battery.
- penang.fandom (Penang War Museum page): built on Punjab Hill. Lists cannon and anti-aircraft firing bays, a command centre, a prison, sleeping quarters, a cookhouse and pillboxes, linked by tunnels.
- MSN travel article: built 1930 to 1939 on about 8 ha.
- malaysia-traveller.com: a 6-inch breech-loading gun stood in a concrete gun firing bay.
- thesmartlocal.my: observation post with a 320° view (see round 2 above).
- travelmalaysia: dark tunnels where visitors crouch, and ladders. The site blocks scripted downloads, so only the search snippet was read.
- The Island Drum (2022 blog): a main covered walkway; dim exhibition rooms off a hallway; the crawl tunnel ends at a ladder to the hilltop with stairs beside it.
- The Planet's Journal (2013 blog): visitor photos and description of the tunnels and cells.
- Axis History Forum: Penang's forts follow the same pattern as the Singapore Naval Base defences.
- Pasir Laba Battery and Heugh Battery pages (fortsiloso archive, read through web.archive.org): two 6-inch emplacements with an underground magazine between them. Used as the template for the gun bays at points 25 and 36.
- Not useful: the UiTM paper (dark tourism, no architecture); Wikipedia (fetch failed); travelfish (blocks downloads); Wikimedia Commons (only the 22 photos already in `photos/`).
- Still wanted: tape measurements of a gun bay, a cell and a barracks room from a site visit, or the museum's own plans (see "Measurements" above).
